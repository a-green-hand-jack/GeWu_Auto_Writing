# Life-sciences manuscript review

For biological, biochemical, medical and related empirical work. Combine with
numerical (statistics) obligations; these rules are domain-specific, not
replacement for a methods specialist.

- Methods must state the model system/organism, sample and control design,
  reagents/instruments, and protocol to the reproducibility standard of the
  target journal. A claim without these is not reviewable.
- Statistics: sample sizes, biological versus technical replicates, uncertainty,
  multiple-comparison correction, blinding/randomization where applicable, and
  any exclusion criteria. P-values alone do not establish effect size or
  practical significance.
- Check unit/identity consistency across figures, tables and text; verify that
  representative images or plots are not mislabeled, cropped to mislead, or
  cherry-picked. Report whether visual inspection was actually possible for the
  selected model.
- Data and code availability, ethics/consent statements, and conflict-of-interest
  disclosure are mandatory obligations, not optional metadata.
- Distinguish association from causation, in-vitro from in-vivo, and observed
  effect from hypothesized mechanism. Do not let application vocabulary force a
  mechanism claim the evidence does not support.
- Sources: literature support must come from inspected primary studies; database
  identifiers and metadata are not content verification.

Report issues with severity and exact figure/table/section location. Missing
statistics, unreported replicates, an overstated causal/mechanism claim, or an
ethics/data-availability gap is critical.
